# Source notes and publication limits

Reference date: 29 October 2026. Sources were reviewed using official public pages/documents, with a mix of parsed retrieval and live-browser access. This is not continuous source monitoring. No full-page government archive is represented as included.

## Explicitly conflict-focused

- The current 2026 National Defence Strategy explicitly includes national civil preparedness and resilience against regional/global disruption.
- The parliamentary inquiry announcement of 4 August 2026 covers continuity of government, essential goods/services and fuel during crisis or conflict. It is an announcement, not a report of findings or an adopted readiness standard.
- September 2026 Australian Government Crisis Management Framework sets government-level responsibilities. It is not a household wartime manual.

## Guidance shared with general emergencies

The website deliberately reuses appropriate household continuity measures while disclosing their original purpose. Water, pantry, kit, generator and medicine advice from Queensland/NSW/Healthdirect is NOT relabelled as newly issued national wartime advice.

- Queensland: three-day kit; at least 10 L drinking water/person over three days; longer supplies for isolation.
- NSW RFS: at least 3 L drinking water/person/day. Calculator uses exactly this rate. A one-week calculation is arithmetic, not a newly sourced federal requirement.
- NSW SES: medicines for five days; includes an add-before-leaving list.
- Queensland's linked checklist: a week's medicine supply.
- NSW Health pantry: up to 14 days at home; longstanding general emergency guidance.
- Water treatment: a boil-water notice is not a do-not-drink notice. Boiling is not a universal treatment for chemical or radiological contamination.
- The RBA recognises cash as a payments backup; it does not prescribe a household dollar figure or guarantee merchant acceptance.
- Life-support registration helps providers plan but cannot prevent every outage.

## Incident boundaries

Escape/Hide/Tell is Australian armed-attacker guidance, not a generic air-raid or fallout procedure. Radiation sheltering/evacuation requires incident-specific public-authority instructions. Potassium iodide is not general radiation protection and should only be used when public authorities advise it.

The sources reviewed do not establish a comprehensive current Australian household wartime manual or a publicly designated national blast/fallout shelter network. This is a limit of the review, NOT a definitive claim that no relevant facilities or classified/non-public plans exist. No ordinary basement, car park, tunnel or historical shelter is designated by this website.

## AusAlert

Official rollout material describes October 2026 availability and phased jurisdictional adoption, with parallel availability of the SMS system until at least July 2027. Research did not independently establish completed nationwide live deployment by 29 October. The site makes this uncertainty explicit rather than treating a launch schedule as proof of universal readiness.

The direct AusAlert launch page was reviewed by the research agent in a browser, but a subsequent parsed fetch returned an error. The source directory instead links the verified NEMA programme page and the July ministerial/NEMA operational statement. It does not claim that the error disproves launch.

## Advice not included
